Ambedkar King Study Circle (AKSC) and San Jose Peace and Justice Center (SJPJC) held a remembrance and public discussion at the 50th anniversary of the Kilvenmani massacre.

The program included a public screening of the documentary _The Hut of Ramiah_ and an open forum discussion.

On 25 December 1968, 44 landless Dalit agricultural labourers, including children and women, were burned to death by the landlords at Kilvenmani village, Nagapattinam district, Tamil Nadu, India. Kilvenmani is not an isolated incident and a memory of the past. Mass violence exercised on the Dalits is a walk of life.

To list a few of them: Saharanpur, Uttar Pradesh (2017); Dharmapuri, Tamil Nadu (2012); Paralipudur, Tamil Nadu (2011); Khairlanji, Maharashtra (2006); Kambalapalli, Karnataka (2000); Melavalavu, Tamil Nadu (1997); Laxmanpur-Bathe, Bihar (1997); Bathani Tola, Bihar (1996); Karamchedu, Andhra Pradesh (1985). The mass violence and ideology of violence against the Dalits can be stopped only by mobilizing the progressive, democratic and secular forces irrespective of the caste identity to challenge such violences in non-violence means.

_Ramiahvin Kudisai_ (_The Hut of Ramiah_) is a documentary directed by **Bharathi Krishnakumar**, released in 2006, based on the gruesome Kilvenmani incident. It documents the series of events which led to the massacre of 44 people and the aftermath of the incident. Following the screening, an open forum discussed how similar atrocities happen against oppressed people even today and what we can do to prevent them.
